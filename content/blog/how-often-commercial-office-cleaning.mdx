**Most small-to-medium offices need professional cleaning 2 to 3 times per week**, though high-traffic workplaces like medical centres and childcare centres often require a daily service. The right schedule for your business comes down to foot traffic, industry, and what's actually at stake if standards slip.

## Factors That Determine Your Cleaning Frequency

- **Foot traffic** — how many staff and visitors move through the space each day
- **Industry** — healthcare, childcare and food service face hygiene requirements a standard office doesn't
- **Floor area and layout** — open-plan spaces and shared kitchens accumulate mess faster than compartmentalised offices
- **Client-facing exposure** — a reception area or showroom needs to look presentable every day, not just after a weekly clean
- **Staff health considerations** — offices with higher sick-day rates often benefit from more frequent disinfection of high-touch surfaces

## Recommended Cleaning Schedule by Business Type

| Business type | Recommended frequency | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Small office (under 10 staff) | 2–3 times per week | Lower foot traffic, but bathrooms and kitchens still need regular attention |
| Open-plan office (10–50 staff) | Daily or 4–5 times per week | Higher traffic through shared spaces, kitchens and meeting rooms |
| Medical or allied health centre | Daily | Strict hygiene standards and continuous public foot traffic |
| Childcare centre | Daily | Health regulations plus close contact between children |
| Retail or showroom | Daily | Customer-facing space needs to look presentable at open each day |
| Warehouse or industrial site | Weekly, with daily spot-cleaning | Larger floor areas but typically lower risk of contamination in office zones |

These are starting points — a business with unusually high visitor numbers or a food-handling component should generally move up a tier from what's listed here.

## What's Included in Commercial Office Cleaning

A standard commercial clean typically covers:

- Vacuuming and mopping all floor areas
- Emptying and re-lining bins throughout the office
- Wiping down desks, benches and high-touch surfaces (door handles, light switches, shared equipment)
- Cleaning and restocking bathrooms
- Kitchen and breakroom cleaning, including sinks and benchtops
- Dusting of common areas and reception

Less frequent, scheduled add-ons usually include carpet steam cleaning, window cleaning, and deep cleaning of kitchens or bathrooms beyond daily maintenance.

## Signs Your Current Schedule Isn't Enough

- Dust or grime is visibly building up again before the next scheduled clean
- Staff or visitors have raised concerns about bathroom cleanliness
- Sick days have crept up, particularly during cold and flu season
- Bins are overflowing between services
- Your reception or entrance doesn't look presentable by the end of the day

If any of these sound familiar, it's usually cheaper in the long run to increase cleaning frequency than to deal with the flow-on effects of an under-cleaned workplace.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How often should a small office be professionally cleaned?**
Most small offices with under 10 staff do well with 2-3 professional cleans per week, covering bins, kitchens, bathrooms and high-touch surfaces, with a full clean weekly.

**Do medical centres and childcare centres need daily cleaning?**
Yes. Facilities with strict hygiene requirements — medical centres, childcare, allied health — generally need daily cleaning to meet health and safety standards, particularly for bathrooms, waiting areas and high-touch surfaces.

**What's included in a standard commercial office clean?**
A standard commercial clean covers vacuuming and mopping floors, emptying bins, wiping down desks and high-touch surfaces, cleaning bathrooms and kitchens, and dusting common areas. Deeper tasks like carpet steam cleaning or window cleaning are usually scheduled separately.

**How do I know if my current cleaning schedule isn't enough?**
Common signs include recurring dust build-up between cleans, complaints about bathroom hygiene, an increase in staff sick days, or visible grime in high-traffic areas like entrances and kitchens.
