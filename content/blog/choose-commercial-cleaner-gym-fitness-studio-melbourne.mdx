**The right commercial cleaner for a gym or fitness studio is one that understands high-touch equipment and wet areas, can clean outside your opening hours, carries proper insurance and agrees a written scope and schedule.** Members judge a gym within seconds of walking in, and a poorly cleaned change room or sticky equipment is the kind of thing they mention in reviews. This guide sets out what to look for, what to ask, and how to compare quotes.

## Why gym cleaning is different

A gym is not an office. Compared with an office, a gym has:

- **High-contact surfaces** such as weights, handles, benches and cardio consoles, touched by many people each day.
- **Wet areas:** showers, change rooms and toilets that need regular scrubbing and sanitising.
- **Specialised flooring:** rubber, vinyl, timber or turf that needs the right products.
- **Mirrors and glass** that show marks quickly.
- **Odour control,** because sweat and damp build up fast.
- **Peak hours,** meaning cleaning needs to fit around classes and busy periods.

A cleaner who only does offices may not plan for these. Ask whether they have worked in gyms, studios or community fitness venues.

## What to look for in a gym cleaner

### 1. A clear scope of work
Ask for a written checklist covering:

- Equipment and touch-point surfaces wiped down
- Gym floors vacuumed and mopped
- Change rooms and lockers cleaned
- Showers, tiles and toilets sanitised
- Mirrors and glass polished
- Bins emptied and reception cleaned

Ask what is included each visit and what is a periodic task, such as deep cleaning grout or cleaning vents.

### 2. After-hours flexibility
You want cleans that do not disrupt members. Typical options are early morning before opening, late at night after close, or between classes. Agree how the cleaner gets access (key, fob, code), how the alarm is handled and who to contact if there is a problem.

### 3. Insurance and screening
Ask for:

- **Public liability insurance** and whether it covers your type of premises.
- **Police-checked staff,** since cleaners often work alone in your venue after hours.
- **Workers compensation** arrangements for their staff.

Uday Cleaning is fully insured and uses police-checked cleaners.

### 4. Appropriate products
Gym surfaces need cleaners that are effective but safe on equipment upholstery, rubber and glass. Ask what products are used and whether the cleaner uses eco-friendly options. Uday Cleaning uses eco-friendly products suited to each surface.

### 5. A reliable schedule and communication
Ask who your point of contact is, how missed cleans are handled and how you report an issue. A simple way to test this is how quickly and clearly a cleaner replies to your first enquiry.

### 6. Reviews and references
Look for reviews from similar venues. If you cannot find any, ask for a reference from another commercial client.

## How often should a gym be cleaned?

There is no single answer, but common patterns are:

| Venue type | Typical frequency |
|---|---|
| Large gym or 24/7 club | Daily, sometimes more than once |
| Boutique studio | Several times a week |
| Yoga or pilates studio | Two to four times a week |
| Martial arts or dance studio | Several times a week |
| Community or council fitness centre | Daily or by booking pattern |

Your own staff will usually wipe equipment between sessions. Professional cleaning covers the deeper clean: floors, wet areas, glass, bins and detailing.

## Questions to ask before you hire

1. Have you cleaned gyms or studios before?
2. What is included in each clean, and what is extra?
3. Can you clean outside our opening hours?
4. How do you handle keys, alarms and access?
5. Are your cleaners police-checked and fully insured?
6. What products do you use on equipment and floors?
7. What happens if we are not happy with a clean?
8. Can you adjust the scope as our membership grows?
9. Is the quote fixed or charged by the hour?
10. Who is our contact if something goes wrong?

## Red flags

- **No written scope.** If a quote is just a price, you do not know what you are buying.
- **No insurance details** when you ask.
- **Pressure to sign a long contract** without a trial period.
- **A quote that is far lower than others** with no explanation. Underpriced cleans often cut time on the wet areas.
- **Slow or unclear communication** before you have even signed.

## How quotes are priced

Commercial cleaning is usually priced by the size of the space, the number of cleans per week, the scope and the time needed. Wet areas and showers add time. Ask for a fixed price per clean or per month based on a clear scope so you can compare quotes fairly. Pricing also depends on your site and access, so we ask for a quick walk-through or a few details before quoting.

## Equipment and floors: what to tell your cleaner

Different gym surfaces need different care, so share these details up front:

- **Rubber and vinyl flooring:** usually damp mopped with a pH-suitable cleaner so the surface is not damaged.
- **Timber studio floors:** avoid excess water, and agree whether your own team or the cleaner handles any specialist treatments.
- **Mirrors and glass:** cleaned regularly to avoid smears, especially in group fitness rooms.
- **Upholstered benches:** wiped with products that will not crack or degrade the vinyl.
- **Turf and functional training areas:** vacuumed or swept to remove loose debris.

If your gym has a pool, sauna or spa, tell your cleaner at the quote stage. Those areas have their own hygiene needs and are often handled under a separate scope or by a specialist.

## Starting well

The first few weeks set the standard. Ask for a walk-through on the first day, agree a short feedback loop and share a contact for urgent issues. Review the checklist after a month and adjust the frequency if your busy periods have changed. A good cleaner will welcome that conversation.
