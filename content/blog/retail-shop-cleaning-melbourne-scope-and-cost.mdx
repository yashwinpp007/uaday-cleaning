**Retail shop cleaning covers the floors, counters, displays, fitting rooms, stockroom, toilets and shopfront glass, usually scheduled around trading hours, and the price depends mainly on floor area, how often the shop is cleaned and what is in the scope.** A tidy shop supports sales, and a clear daily and weekly split keeps costs sensible. This guide sets out what to clean and when, what drives the price, and how to compare quotes.

## Why shop cleaning matters

Customers read cleanliness as a sign of care. Grimy floors, smeared glass and dusty displays make products look worse. Clean shops are also safer for staff and customers, with fewer slip and trip hazards. Presentation matters most at the entrance, so that is where to start.

## What a shop clean covers

A typical retail clean includes:

- **Shopfront and internal glass** wiped streak-free.
- **Floors** vacuumed, swept and mopped.
- **Counters and the till area** wiped and tidy.
- **Displays and shelving** dusted.
- **Fitting rooms,** mirrors, handles and hooks.
- **Staff areas and stockrooms** cleaned.
- **Toilets** cleaned and sanitised.
- **Bins** emptied and the entrance kept presentable.

## Daily vs weekly scope

Splitting tasks by frequency keeps your cleaning effective without paying for deep work every day.

### Daily (or every trading day)
1. Empty bins.
2. Vacuum, sweep and mop floors.
3. Wipe counters, the till and touch points.
4. Clean toilets and restock on request.
5. Spot-clean the shopfront glass and door handles.
6. Tidy fitting rooms.

### Weekly
1. Dust shelves, displays and ledges.
2. Clean the full shopfront and internal glass.
3. Wash and detail floors in problem areas.
4. Clean the stockroom and staff room.
5. Wipe skirting boards, doors and frames.

### Monthly or as needed
1. Deep clean floors or grout.
2. Clean vents, light fittings and high areas.
3. Wash walls and remove marks.
4. Spot-treat carpet or machine-scrub hard floors.

## What drives the cost

There is no flat rate for shop cleaning, because every shop is different. These factors decide the quote:

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Floor area | More area means more time |
| Frequency | Daily, several times a week or weekly |
| Scope | Floors only, or glass, toilets and stockroom |
| Floor types | Carpet, tiles, timber or polished concrete need different methods |
| Fitting rooms and toilets | Each adds detail work |
| Access | After-hours entry, keys, alarms and building rules |
| Add-ons | Window cleaning, pressure washing or periodic deep cleans |
| Condition | A first clean of a neglected shop takes longer |

Ask for a written scope with a fixed price per clean or per month. Do not compare quotes on price alone if the scope differs.

Most shops prefer a clean when customers are not there. Options include early morning before opening, after close in the evening, or overnight. ## Employment and award rates

Cleaning work is covered by Australian workplace laws and awards, which affect what reputable providers must pay their staff. This is one reason a very low quote can be a warning sign. For general information see the [Fair Work Ombudsman](https://www.fairwork.gov.au).

## Cleaning by floor type

The right method depends on what is underfoot, so tell your cleaner what you have:

- **Carpet:** vacuumed regularly, with periodic steam cleaning where it is heavily used. See our [carpet cleaning service](/services/carpet-cleaning).
- **Tiles and polished concrete:** swept and mopped, with grout checked for build-up in wet entrances.
- **Timber and laminate:** damp mopped rather than soaked, to protect the finish.
- **Vinyl:** mopped with a suitable cleaner and checked for scuffs.

Entry mats and the first few metres inside the door take the most wear, especially on wet days. Cleaning those areas more often keeps dirt from being tracked through the shop.

## Seasonal and event cleans

Retail has busy and quiet periods. Many shops add a deeper clean before a sale, a stocktake, a new season's range or a refit. After a fit-out, a builders clean removes construction dust before stock goes on the shelves. ## A simple way to compare quotes

1. Give every cleaner the same details: floor area, floor types, toilets, fitting rooms and frequency.
2. Ask each to put the scope in writing.
3. Compare the price per clean, then the total per month.
4. Check what is excluded, such as glass or the stockroom.
5. Ask how problems are fixed. Reputable cleaners will return and correct anything missed.

If a quote looks too good to be true, look at what has been left out.
