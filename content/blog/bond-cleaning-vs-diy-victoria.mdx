**In Victoria, renters must leave the property reasonably clean and in the same condition as when they moved in, except for fair wear and tear. A professional clean is often the easiest way to meet that standard, but it is not automatically required by law.** What matters is the result at the final inspection and what your lease says. This guide explains your obligations in plain English, when DIY works, and when paying for a professional clean is worth it.

*This is general information, not legal advice. For your own situation, check your lease and Consumer Affairs Victoria's guidance.*

## What the law says in plain English

The Residential Tenancies Act 1997 (Vic) sets out the rights and duties of renters and rental providers. For cleaning, the practical effect is:

- You must **leave the property reasonably clean** when you move out.
- You must **leave it in the same condition as at the start**, apart from fair wear and tear.
- You are not responsible for normal ageing, such as faded paint or worn carpet in a high-traffic area.
- Your **entry condition report** is the reference point. It is the record of how the property looked when you moved in.

You can read Consumer Affairs Victoria's guidance at [consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting) and find the Act on the [Victorian Legislation website](https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au).

## What "reasonably clean" looks like at inspection

There is no single government checklist. In practice, agents look at:

- The kitchen: oven, cooktop, rangehood, benches, sink and cupboards.
- Bathrooms and toilets: shower, tiles, grout, mirrors and fixtures.
- Floors: vacuumed, mopped and free of stains.
- Windows and tracks, light fittings and walls.
- Whether anything is left behind.

Many agents use a checklist based on the Real Estate Institute of Victoria (REIV) standard. Our [end of lease cleaning checklist](/blog/end-of-lease-cleaning-checklist-melbourne) shows the typical room-by-room items.

## How the bond is returned

Your bond is held by the Residential Tenancies Bond Authority (RTBA), not the landlord. At the end of the tenancy a claim is lodged and both parties generally agree on how the bond is split. If you and the rental provider cannot agree, either side can take the dispute to VCAT. You can read about the process at [rtba.vic.gov.au](https://www.rtba.vic.gov.au).

A well-documented clean helps in two ways: it lowers the chance of a dispute and, if one happens, gives you evidence. Take dated photos of every room after cleaning and keep your receipts.

## DIY cleaning: when it works

Cleaning yourself can be the right choice when:

- The property is small and was kept in good condition.
- You have the time, a few days of flexibility and the right products.
- Your lease does not require professional carpet cleaning.
- You are confident with the oven, grout and window tracks, which are the hard parts.

### DIY tips
1. **Start with the entry condition report** and aim to return each room to that standard.
2. **Work top to bottom:** ceilings and fans, then walls, then benches, then floors last.
3. **Do the oven early** since it takes the longest. Use a suitable oven cleaner and follow the label.
4. **Descale tapware and shower screens** with a product suited to the surface.
5. **Clean tracks** with a small brush, then wipe.
6. **Photograph everything** when you finish.

## Professional bond cleaning: when it makes sense

A professional clean is worth considering when:

- **You are short on time** because of work, travel or moving day.
- **The property is large** or has multiple bathrooms.
- **The oven, grout or carpets are in poor condition** and need specialist work.
- **Your lease requires a professional receipt**, such as for carpet steam cleaning.
- **You want certainty,** especially if your bond is a large sum.
- **You are moving interstate** and cannot return for a re-clean.

## Carpet cleaning: check your lease

Carpets are the item most often misunderstood. Many leases ask for professional steam cleaning, and agents may want a receipt. If your lease does not mention it, you must still return the carpet in the same condition, apart from fair wear and tear. When in doubt, ask your agent in writing. ## Protect your bond whichever you choose

1. Read your lease and entry condition report before you start.
2. Keep communication with your agent in writing.
3. Photograph the property after cleaning, dated if possible.
4. Keep every receipt and invoice.
5. Attend the final inspection if you can.
6. Do not sign off on a bond claim you disagree with. Seek advice from Consumer Affairs Victoria or a tenant advocacy service.
